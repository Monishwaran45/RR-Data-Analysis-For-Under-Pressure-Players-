Critical Methodological Breakthrough

Why Arbitrary Over Filtering (Overs 16–20) Fails in Cricket Analytics

Traditional scouting models frequently define "pressure" merely as the death overs (overs 16–20). This is mathematically flawed and leads to catastrophic bidding errors:

Scenario A: Early Collapse Chase (Over 4, Score 14/3)

Target 210, Required Run Rate = 12.25 RPO, 3 frontline wickets down. True Situational Pressure = 88.4 (EXTREME). Traditional models classify this as "casual Powerplay" and discard it, ignoring batters who anchor and fight back.

Scenario B: Run-A-Ball Dead Rubber (Over 19, Score 180/2)

Chasing 184, needing 4 runs off 12 balls with 8 wickets in hand. True Situational Pressure = 12.1 (LOW). Traditional death-over filters erroneously label this as "high pressure death batting", distorting player valuations.

Taxonomy of Pressure

The 4 High-Pressure Regimes in T20 Cricket (PI ≥ 65)

1. 2nd-Innings Chase Crunch

Triggered when $RRR \ge 10.0$ or $(RRR - CRR) \ge 2.50$, scaled by balls remaining and wickets lost. Every dot ball exponentially increases delivery leverage.

2. Top-Order Collapse Recovery

Triggered when $\le 35$ runs are scored with $\ge 3$ wickets lost in the first 6 overs. Requires elite composure to prevent a complete batting blackout while maintaining boundary threat.

3. 1st-Innings Death Acceleration

Innings 1, Overs 16–20 with $\le 5$ wickets down. Batters face extreme pressure to elevate above par expectations without surrendering the tail.

4. Low-Target Defend Choke

Defending totals $< 160$ where bowlers must maintain an economy rate below 6.50 and strike continuously to stay in the contest.
